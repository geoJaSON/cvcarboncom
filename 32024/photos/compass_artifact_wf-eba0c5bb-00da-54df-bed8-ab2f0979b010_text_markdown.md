# Requirements & Gap-Analysis Framework for an Independent International CDR Crediting Program (mCDR + DAC)

## TL;DR
- To be treated as high-integrity for durable removals internationally, your program must clear five stacked bars: ICVCM Core Carbon Principles (program + methodology "two-tick"), CORSIA Emissions Unit Eligibility Criteria (the ICVCM floor), ISO 14065/17029-accredited third-party VVBs, robust registry controls preventing double issuance/use/claiming (ideally CAD Trust-connected), and - for anything crossing borders toward another NDC or CORSIA - host-country authorization plus a corresponding adjustment under Article 6. Your certified methodology and working registry are necessary but not sufficient; the gaps for new entrants are almost always in *program-level governance evidence*, *conservative uncertainty/permanence quantification*, and *legal/permitting documentation for ocean interventions*.
- The single hardest technical requirement for you is removals-specific MRV: mCDR (OAE, seaweed sinking) has genuinely contested science - air-sea CO₂ equilibration takes years and spans thousands of km, forcing reliance on ocean models plus conservative uncertainty discounting; DAC is easier to quantify but turns on additionality (45Q/EU subsidy "would it happen anyway"), lifecycle/grid emissions accounting, and Class VI/geologic storage verification with tax-credit double-counting risk.
- The biggest *legal* constraint unique to your marine credits: the 2013 London Protocol marine-geoengineering amendment (LP.4(8)) is **not yet in force** - only nine Parties have accepted it (Australia, Croatia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, UK) versus the 37-of-55 two-thirds threshold needed - and its Annex 4 lists only ocean fertilization, so mCDR governance is fragmented; the BBNJ/High Seas Treaty entered into force **17 January 2026** and imposes environmental impact assessment duties on high-seas activity. Your registry must require documented national permits and EIAs as a condition of crediting.

## Key Findings

**1. There is now a de facto integrity "stack," and ICVCM sits at its center.** The Integrity Council's Core Carbon Principles (CCP) Assessment Framework is the reference threshold that VCMI (demand side), rating agencies, and buyers all point to. As of the 20 April 2026 batch, ICVCM had approved ten programs as CCP-Eligible (ACR, ART TREES, CAR, Equitable Earth, Gold Standard, GCC, Isometric, Puro.earth, Rainbow and Verra) and roughly 40 methodologies as CCP-Approved while rejecting ~25 (by a 4 August 2026 update the counts had risen to 13 programs and 41 approved / 25 rejected methodologies) - a signal that meeting the bar is genuinely hard. Both a CDR-only registry (Isometric) and a durable-removals registry (Puro.earth) are already approved, so there is a proven path for your profile.

**2. CORSIA is the implicit floor beneath ICVCM.** The CCP Assessment Framework repeatedly phrases its criteria as "*in addition to* CORSIA requirements," so a program that cannot meet CORSIA's Emissions Unit Eligibility Criteria (EUC) cannot meet the CCPs. Eight programs currently supply CORSIA-eligible units for the 2024-2026 first phase; the 2025 re-assessment cycle (for 2027-2029) drew applications from ACR, ART, Gold Standard and Verra, plus material-change submissions including Isometric. Approval is by the ICAO Council on recommendation of the Technical Advisory Body (TAB).

**3. Removals-specific requirements are converging fast.** The Article 6.4 Supervisory Body's "Requirements for activities involving removals" (SBM014, in force 9 October 2024) plus the EU CRCF Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 (QU.A.L.ITY criteria) and the CDR-native standards (Puro.earth, Isometric) now define what "durable removal" crediting must contain: minimum durability, reversal-risk assessment tools, buffer pools / pre-issuance deductions, conservative uncertainty treatment, and post-crediting monitoring.

**4. The marine international-law overlay is your distinctive risk.** No unified regulatory regime governs mCDR; the binding instruments (London Protocol amendment) are not in force, and high-seas activity now triggers BBNJ EIA obligations. This makes documented legal compliance/permitting a gating credit-eligibility requirement, not an afterthought.

## Details

### Domain A - Institutional & Governance Requirements

**A.1 ICVCM Core Carbon Principles - the "two-tick" architecture.** Credits can only carry the CCP label if (i) the *program* is CCP-Eligible and (ii) the *methodology/Category* is CCP-Approved. The Assessment Framework (January 2024, v2) splits requirements into Part I (program) and Part II (Category):

Program-level criteria (Section 4, Part I):
- **Criterion 1 – Effective governance** (1.1): independent board with fiduciary duty and robust bylaws; published annual report with revenues/expenses/net assets; corporate social/environmental responsibility processes; robust anti-money-laundering; anti-bribery/anti-corruption practices. Criterion 1.2 requires transparent local + global stakeholder consultation with public comment and a clear, impartial grievance process whose fees do not impede civil society or Indigenous Peoples & Local Communities (IP&LCs).
- **Criterion 2 – Tracking** (2.1): operate/use a registry that uniquely identifies and tracks credits; identify the entity on whose behalf a credit is retired and the retirement purpose; and have procedures to address *erroneous issuance* (cancellation/replacement) with named responsible entities.
- **Criterion 3 – Transparency** (3.1): publish, per activity, all documentation needed for third parties to replicate the baseline/additionality/quantification calculations (including spreadsheets), plus a mitigation activity design document with a non-technical summary, location, proponents, technologies, environmental/social impacts and methodology.
- **Criterion 4 – Robust independent third-party validation & verification** (4.1): require VVBs accredited to a recognized international standard (e.g., current ISO 14065 and ISO 14066, or CDM/Article 6.4 rules); operate a VVB performance-management process with power to suspend/revoke VVBs and report poor performance to accreditation bodies.
- **Criterion 5 – Robust quantification**: methodology approval process (5.1) requires new/major-revision methodologies to undergo independent expert review *and* public stakeholder consultation, plus procedures to suspend/withdraw methodologies shown to over-credit. Criterion 5.2 requires defining a credit as 1 tCO₂e; disclosing GWP values; defining crediting periods; and, critically for you, a formal **overall-uncertainty assessment** combining all causes (assumptions, models, parameters, measurement accuracy) and a "systematic approach to ensuring conservativeness." Criterion 5.3: **ex-ante issued credits are not CCP-eligible** - removals must be issued ex-post.
- **Criterion 6 – No double counting**: 6.1 no double issuance/registration; 6.2 registry provisions preventing further transfer/retirement of an already-retired/cancelled credit.
- **Criterion 7 – Sustainable development benefits & safeguards**: require proponents to abide by national/local law and relevant international conventions; assess environmental/social risks; run **FPIC** for IP&LCs and inclusive local stakeholder consultation; and specifically to "avoid, or where not feasible minimise, negative impacts on terrestrial and *marine* biodiversity and ecosystems" (Criterion 7.5) - directly relevant to ocean interventions.

Category-level criteria (Part II): Criterion 8 (additionality), 9 (permanence), 10 (robust quantification), 11 (no double counting), 12-13 (SD benefits/net-zero contribution). ICVCM has flagged that category-level risk-assessment and SDG-impact-measurement criteria are still being developed in the next AF iteration (noted in Perspectives Climate Group's 2024 CCP/AF analysis).

Application process: programs apply via the ICVCM Assessment Platform; the Council confirms receipt, checks completeness (completed applications are published), assesses against the AF, and the Governing Board makes the formal decision. ICVCM assesses programs internally and Categories via Multi-Stakeholder Working Groups. Note ICVCM has **not yet published CCP approvals for mCDR (OAE, seaweed) methodologies** - Isometric's approved methodologies to date are terrestrial (reforestation, mangrove restoration, IFM) - so you would be at or near the frontier of a marine-CDR CCP assessment.

**A.2 ISO standards - who they bind.**
- *Bind your VVBs (not your program directly):* ISO 14065:2020 (now paired with **ISO/IEC 17029:2019** - general requirements for validation/verification bodies) sets requirements for the bodies; ISO 14066 sets competence requirements for validation/verification teams; ISO 14064-3 sets the requirements for conducting the verification engagement. VVBs are accredited to these by national accreditation bodies (UKAS, ANAB, etc.) operating under **ISO/IEC 17011**.
- *Relevant to methodology/quantification:* ISO 14064-2 (project-level GHG quantification/monitoring/reporting) and 14064-1 (organizational inventories).
- *ISO/IEC 17065* (product certification) is the model where the standard-setter/certifier certifies a "product"; some schemes structure conformity this way.
- *ISO 14068-1:2023* (carbon neutrality) is a demand-side/claims standard - relevant to your buyers' neutrality claims, not your issuance.
Your program demonstrates conformity by *requiring* accredited VVBs in its rules (satisfying CCP 4.1) and by mapping its own quantification methodology to ISO 14064-2 principles.

**A.3 VCMI complementarity.** VCMI's Claims Code of Practice governs the *demand* side (what a buyer may claim - Silver/Gold/Platinum tiers, beyond-value-chain mitigation), and it defines "high-quality" by reference to ICVCM CCPs. Implication for you: buyers pursuing VCMI claims will demand CCP-labelled credits, so CCP-eligibility is effectively a commercial prerequisite, and your registry should support the retirement metadata (beneficiary, purpose) VCMI's Monitoring, Reporting & Assurance framework expects.

**A.4 CORSIA EUC / TAB.** Programs must meet both the "Program Design Elements" criteria and the "Carbon Offset Credit Integrity Assessment" criteria at the program level. Units must be real, additional, permanent, verified, and not double-counted, and must avoid double counting through host-country attestations. For 2021+ vintages, CORSIA eligibility now effectively requires Article 6 authorization and corresponding-adjustment accounting (see A.6). Whether marine CDR/DAC removals qualify: CORSIA does not categorically exclude removals, but no removals-only novel-CDR program has yet been approved specifically for those pathways; your realistic near-term route is ICVCM first, CORSIA later.

**A.5 Article 6.4 (PACM) removals & methodology standards.** SBM014 "Requirements for activities involving removals" (in force 9 Oct 2024) plus the methodology standard and an additionality standard (Feb 2025) require: monitoring via field measurement, remote sensing, instrumentation and/or modelling; **post-crediting-period monitoring** and remediation of reversals; a **reversal-risk assessment tool** producing a percentage risk rating; and a **Reversal Risk Buffer Pool Account** in the mechanism registry (buffer composition published annually, stress-tested at least every 3 years). The Methodological Expert Panel recommended a single buffer-contribution value in the range **0.5%–2.5% over a 100-year timeframe** starting no earlier than the end of the last crediting period. Missing a monitoring/reversal report is deemed a full avoidable reversal. These are the most authoritative international removals rules and a strong template even if you never seek 6.4 registration.

**A.6 Article 6.2 corresponding adjustments - how an *independent* registry interfaces.** By default an independent-registry credit is NOT authorized under Article 6 and carries NO corresponding adjustment (CA). For a credit to count toward another country's NDC or toward CORSIA, the **host country must issue a Letter of Authorization and apply a corresponding adjustment** in its national accounting; the CA is triggered at "first transfer" (clarified at COP29, Nov 2024) and reported via the agreed electronic format into the UNFCCC Article 6 Database / Centralized Accounting and Reporting Platform and the country's Biennial Transparency Report. Independent registries sit *outside* the UN infrastructure and interface by (a) tracking LoA/CA status via registry labels and (b) providing host-country reporting tools. Live gap: as of late 2025 (CMA document L.12) the Article 6.2 international registry infrastructure was still not fully operational. The Article 6.4 **interim registry launched February 2025** (an offline secretariat tool; post-issuance authorization not available in it); the full mechanism registry procurement was cancelled in December 2024 and remained pending. There is **no automatic "conversion"** of Verra/Gold-Standard-type credits into Article 6.4 units - only CDM activities transition into PACM; independent credits enter Article 6 accounting only via sovereign authorization + CA layered on top. Concrete precedents: Verra added Article 6 labels in VCS v4.5 (Aug 2023), maintaining "Article 6 Authorized – International Mitigation Purposes" and "Article 6 Correspondingly Adjusted" labels; Gold Standard committed to registry modifications to identify authorized/adjusted credits and track use-purpose; the two launched a joint Article 6.2 reporting tool to help governments prepare 2026 Biennial Transparency Reports.

**A.7 EU CRCF.** Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 (in force 9 December 2024) creates the first EU-wide voluntary certification framework, with QU.A.L.ITY criteria: **QU**antification (accurate, net climate benefit), **A**dditionality (beyond legal requirements/standard practice), **L**ong-term storage (durability tiers, minimize reversal), and sustainabil**ITY** (do-no-harm to biodiversity, water, marine resources, circular economy). It defines durability tiers - permanent removal (centuries; DACCS, BECCS, biochar), carbon storage in products (≥35 years), carbon farming (≥5 years). It mandates third-party verification, publication in an EU Union registry for carbon removals, and Commission recognition of certification schemes. The Commission adopted the first delegated act with methodologies for permanent removals in February 2026, and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2358 (Nov 2025) set rules on certification schemes/bodies/audits; first certifications are expected from ~2027. DAC (DACCS) is squarely covered; marine CDR is not yet among the first CRCF methodologies. The EU Green Claims Directive and Empowering Consumers Directive further constrain how buyers may market credit-based claims in the EU.

### Domain B - Registry Infrastructure & Operations

**B.1 Core registry controls.** A credible registry needs: unique serialization of every credit; issuance/transfer/retirement/cancellation transaction types; buffer-pool accounts; account types with KYC/AML onboarding (CCP 1.1 requires program-level AML); immutable audit trails; public transparency of project docs, monitoring/verification reports, issuance and retirement records and buffer holdings (CCP 3.1); API access; business-continuity/disaster-recovery; and information security. Vendors typically evidence security via **SOC 2** and **ISO/IEC 27001**; e.g., Xpansiv states its platform is SOC 2-certified with MFA and per-transaction auditability. Puro.earth runs a Nasdaq-powered registry; Isometric, Verra and others run their own.

**B.2 Preventing double counting** requires enforcing all three failure modes: double issuance (CCP 6.1 - no registering an activity active under another program), double use (CCP 6.2 - locking retired/cancelled units), and double claiming (Article 6 CA layer). Interoperability: connect to the World Bank/IETA/Singapore **Climate Action Data Trust (CAD Trust)** - a decentralized, blockchain-based metadata layer harmonizing registry data to detect double counting; connection is free, open-source and uses the **CAD Trust Data Model (v2.0 launched 2025)** with a v2.0 API; roughly 12 registries were connected with full v2.0 transition targeted for Q2 2026. Connecting is a low-cost, high-signal integrity move for a new registry.

**B.3 Legal nature of the credit.** There is no global consensus: a properly issued voluntary credit is likely a *documentary intangible / personal property right* under English law and comparable common-law jurisdictions (Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia); *may or may not* be property in the US (state-dependent) or a financial instrument in the EU (per ISDA and World Bank PPPLRC analyses). Practical implication: because legal certainty is absent, your registry **Terms of Use** must define the credit, the environmental attribute, transfer of title, retirement effect, and choice-of-law/dispute-resolution expressly (as Verra's registry Terms of Use do). This matters especially for buffer-pool ownership and insurance.

**B.4 Independence & conflict-of-interest firewalls.** ICVCM CCP 1.1 requires an independent board; best practice separates the standard-setter, the registry operator, project developers and VVBs. Many programs outsource registry *infrastructure* to neutral vendors (Xpansiv/APX powers 15+ registries including ACR; Xpansiv acquired APX in 2022) while retaining governance - a legitimate way for a small program to get enterprise-grade infrastructure without owning the conflict of building and self-auditing its own ledger. VVBs must be independent of proponents and accredited (CCP 4.1); the program must not verify its own projects.

### Domain C - Integrity Documentation (the artifacts you must publish)

A credible program publishes, at minimum: (1) program rules/standard document; (2) governance charter + board/advisory structure (Puro.earth, e.g., publishes an independent 3-7 member advisory board structure); (3) methodology approval procedure with independent expert review + public consultation (CCP 5.1c); (4) public consultation/stakeholder-consultation policy (CCP 1.2); (5) validation & verification standard; (6) VVB accreditation & approval procedure with performance management (CCP 4.1); (7) conflict-of-interest policy; (8) grievance & appeals mechanism (CCP 1.2) and whistleblower policy; (9) environmental & social safeguards framework incl. FPIC (CCP 7); (10) risk-management and reversal/buffer-pool policy; (11) credit issuance & retirement procedures (with erroneous-issuance remediation, CCP 2.1); (12) fee schedule; (13) registry Terms of Use; (14) data-transparency policy; (15) annual report with financials (CCP 1.1). "Program integrity" evidence in practice = independent oversight board, third-party program-level assessment/accreditation, published assessment reports, error/reversal disclosure logs, cancellation/correction procedures, and independent rating-agency scrutiny.

Rating agencies (BeZero, Sylvera, Calyx Global, Renoster, MSCI) rate at credit/project level, but their frameworks probe program and methodology quality: additionality, carbon accounting, permanence/non-permanence risk, baseline credibility, co-benefits. The three highest-volume raters (BeZero, Sylvera, Calyx) have converged on an AAA–D scale (Calyx moved to it Jan 2025); Calyx explicitly evaluates at program, methodology and project levels and tracks ICVCM CCP eligibility. BeZero maintains a strict independence policy (no trading, project development or MRV consulting). Expect raters to scrutinize your uncertainty discounting and durability claims hardest for mCDR.

### Domain D - Removals-Specific Requirements (mCDR + DAC)

**D.1 Durability / permanence tiering.** Market benchmarks: Puro.earth requires **100-year minimum** durability (raised from 50 years in Jan 2023), labels CORCs as 100+, 200+ and 1,000+ years, and uses a **pre-issuance deduction** for expected degradation/reversal instead of a separate buffer. EU CRCF uses "centuries" for permanent removal. Geologic DAC/mineralization is generally treated as 1,000+ years (IPCC: fraction retained after 1,000 years for mineral carbonation "virtually certain to be 100%"). OAE stores carbon as dissolved bicarbonate with residence times often cited at 10,000+ years, but *seaweed sinking durability is depth- and location-dependent*: recent modelling (Sten et al., 2026, *Earth's Future*) finds seaweed sinking can approach DAC-like efficiency and >100-year durability only if biomass is conveyed rapidly (≥1,000 m/day) to depth (≥2,000 m); shallower/slower sinking sharply reduces durability. Your program must tier durability by pathway and apply reversal treatment (buffer pool vs. pre-issuance deduction vs. insurance/delivery guarantee) accordingly.

**D.2 mCDR MRV - the central scientific challenge.** Direct measurement of ocean carbon uptake is very hard: air-sea CO₂ equilibration takes years and disperses up to thousands of km from the site. The leading approach (Isometric OAE protocol, informed by [C]Worthy's open-source tools) combines site measurements with internationally recognized numerical ocean models to quantify additional drawdown, and requires: full cradle-to-ocean lifecycle emissions accounting; explicit uncertainty disclosure (projects commonly report ±20-30% and issue conservatively, discounting for uncertainty); conservative treatment of loss terms (e.g., secondary carbonate precipitation) via guardrails, conservative upper-bound estimates, and public disclosure of all data. Copernicus/State Planet's OAE MRV work stresses that robust MRV requires intensive observation over large areas/long times and that even pH sensor accuracy (0.01 pH ≈ 10 µatm pCO₂) is a limiting factor. Reference frameworks: the Frontier/CarbonPlan "Verification Confidence Level" mapping, Carbon Direct/Microsoft "Criteria for High-Quality CDR," the OAE "Guide to Best Practices" (Copernicus 2023), and the Aspen Institute mCDR research code of conduct. NGO/scientific criticism (Carbon Market Watch, Sylvera, WRI, Mongabay) centers on early-stage science, ecological uncertainty, and premature commercial crediting - so conservative crediting and staged issuance are essential.

**D.3 DAC MRV & additionality.** DAC quantification is more tractable but hinges on: energy accounting and LCA boundaries (electrified vs. thermal DAC; net removal after grid emissions); **additionality** given large subsidies - under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed 4 July 2025) US 45Q pays **$180/tonne for direct air capture in dedicated geologic storage** (with parity so utilization/EOR now also qualify at $180/t for DAC; inflation-indexed from 2027, base year 2025), raising a genuine "would it happen anyway" question, and EU funding similarly; grid-emissions accounting for the power consumed; **storage verification** under EPA **Class VI** wells / geologic-storage regulation; and avoiding **double counting of the environmental attribute** between the 45Q tax credit and the issued carbon credit. 45Q carries a 3-year recapture tail tied to storage performance and "no double dipping" rules - your program must decide and document whether 45Q-supported tonnes can also be credited, and how additionality survives the subsidy.

**D.4 Marine international-law constraints (documentation you must require).**
- **London Convention/Protocol:** The 2013 marine-geoengineering amendment (Resolution **LP.4(8)**) is **not in force** - only nine Parties have accepted it (Australia, Croatia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, UK) versus the 37-of-55 two-thirds threshold; Annex 4 currently lists only ocean fertilization. Ocean fertilization other than legitimate scientific research is disallowed (2008 resolution). Parties issued non-binding statements (2023, 2024) recommending OAE and biomass sinking be treated cautiously and largely confined to legitimate scientific research; a 2024 draft resolution (LC 46/LP 19) was not adopted; work on definitions and an expert roster continued through LC 47/LP 20 (Oct 2025), where 56 Parties to the Protocol were recorded. Sub-seabed CO₂ storage is permitted (2006 amendment, in force 2007); transboundary CO₂ *export* (2009 LP.3(4)) is not in force but provisionally applicable (LP.5(14), 2019).
- **UNCLOS** imposes general duties to protect the marine environment.
- **BBNJ / High Seas Treaty:** adopted 19 June 2023; Sierra Leone and Morocco became the 60th and 61st ratifiers on 19 September 2025, triggering **entry into force on 17 January 2026** (Article 68(1), 120 days after the 60th instrument); imposes environmental impact assessment (EIA) requirements and area-based management tools on activities in areas beyond national jurisdiction - directly applicable to high-seas mCDR.
- **Convention on Biological Diversity:** moratorium-style guidance on climate geoengineering except small-scale controlled research.
Practical requirement: your registry must make credit eligibility conditional on documented **national permits, EIAs, and legal-compliance attestations** appropriate to the deployment location (national waters vs. high seas), and should default to precaution where binding international rules are absent.

**D.5 Ocean-specific safeguards.** Beyond CCP 7.5 (marine biodiversity), require ecological risk assessment, marine ecosystem-impact monitoring, and consultation with coastal and Indigenous communities including FPIC (CCP 7.1). Ocean Visions, NASEM reports, NOAA guidance and the London Protocol assessment framework (Annex 5 risk assessment) are the reference bodies.

## Highest-risk / most commonly failed requirements for new entrants
1. **Weak or non-conservative uncertainty quantification** - the fastest route to a downgrade or greenwashing critique for mCDR; regulators and raters expect explicit uncertainty ranges and conservative discounting.
2. **Additionality under subsidy** - DAC tonnes already earning 45Q ($180/t) or EU grants face "would it happen anyway" challenges and attribute double-counting.
3. **Ex-ante issuance** - disqualifies CCP eligibility outright.
4. **Thin safeguards / no FPIC / no marine EIA** - ICVCM has noted category-level safeguards are underdeveloped market-wide; ocean interventions raise the bar.
5. **Self-verification or non-accredited VVBs** - violates CCP 4.1.
6. **Registry double-counting gaps** - not CAD-Trust-connected, no locked retirements.
7. **Legal-nature ambiguity** - Terms of Use that don't clearly define title/transfer/retirement.

## Recommendations (staged)

**Stage 0 - Gap self-assessment (now, 1-2 months).** Run the checklist above against your methodology, workflow and registry. Prioritize the seven high-risk items. Map every methodology clause to CCP 5/8/9, ISO 14064-2, and (for DAC) CRCF QU.A.L.ITY and (for marine) London Protocol/BBNJ. Benchmark thresholds: aim for explicit uncertainty ranges with conservative issuance, ex-post-only issuance, and a durability tier per pathway.

**Stage 1 - Foundational integrity (0-6 months).** Stand up/verify: independent board + COI policy; public-consultation + grievance + whistleblower policies; VVB accreditation requirement (ISO 14065/17029/14066); erroneous-issuance remediation; retirement-beneficiary/purpose capture; Terms of Use. Connect to **CAD Trust** (free, fast, high integrity signal). Obtain/confirm **SOC 2 / ISO 27001** for the registry (build vs. partner with an Xpansiv/APX-type vendor - recommended for a small team to avoid conflicts and the cost of self-building).

**Stage 2 - Removals-specific rigor (3-9 months).** Finalize: reversal-risk tool + buffer/pre-issuance-deduction policy sized to risk; post-crediting monitoring; durability tiering; mCDR model-based MRV with [C]Worthy-style tooling and public data; DAC LCA/grid-emissions boundary and 45Q attribute policy; marine permit/EIA eligibility gates. Seek independent rating (BeZero/Sylvera/Calyx) as external validation.

**Stage 3 - Formal recognition, in order (6-24 months).**
1. **ICVCM CCP-Eligibility** (program) - the anchor; then push your mCDR/DAC methodologies through Category assessment (you'd be at the frontier for marine).
2. **CORSIA TAB** (once CCP-aligned) for aviation-compliance demand.
3. **EU CRCF** scheme recognition (esp. for DAC/DACCS) as EU methodologies mature (~2027).
4. **Article 6 labeling capability** if buyers need NDC/CORSIA use - build LoA/CA tracking like Verra v4.5.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** if ICVCM publishes an mCDR Category decision, align to it immediately; if the London Protocol LP.4(8) amendment enters into force or Annex 4 is expanded to OAE/biomass, treat those rules as binding gates; if CRCF adopts a marine methodology, pursue recognition.

**Build vs. partner:** A small independent registry can realistically own governance, methodology, safeguards and MRV rules, but should **partner for registry infrastructure** (Xpansiv/APX, Nasdaq-type, or equivalent) rather than self-build, and **seek recognition** (ICVCM, CRCF) rather than replicate everything. Owning the ledger while also setting standards and verifying is exactly the conflict raters and ICVCM scrutinize.

## Caveats
- **Fast-moving/contested areas:** ICVCM's next AF iteration will add category-level risk and SDG criteria; the Article 6.2/6.4 registries are not yet fully operational (interim 6.4 registry launched Feb 2025; full registry procurement cancelled Dec 2024); CRCF marine methodologies don't yet exist; and mCDR science is genuinely unsettled - treat all durability/efficiency numbers as pathway- and location-dependent estimates, not settled facts.
- **No mCDR CCP precedent yet:** ICVCM has approved terrestrial Isometric methodologies but not, to date, an OAE or seaweed-sinking Category - you would be near the frontier, which raises both risk and first-mover value.
- **Legal-nature and international-law uncertainty** means contractual documentation and precautionary permitting carry more weight than usual.
- Some figures (BBNJ ratification counts, London Protocol acceptance counts, ICVCM approval tallies) are moving targets; verify against IMO, UNFCCC and ICVCM primary sources at the time you file. As of the reporting date, BBNJ had 60+ ratifications and entered into force 17 Jan 2026; the London Protocol LP.4(8) amendment had nine acceptances against a 37-of-55 threshold; and ICVCM listed 10-13 CCP-Eligible programs depending on the 2026 batch cited.
